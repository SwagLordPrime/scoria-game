## 1. Premise
A war devastated the planet — a war so long it stopped being an event and became **a way of
life**: generations born, raised, and buried inside it; an endless war almost no one
remembered starting. The chronicles call it **the Melancholy Wars** — just **"the
Melancholy"** in common speech. The Black Feast alone calls it **the Craving**, because
they alone know whose craving it was (§3c). The peace that finally came was not won by treaty alone — **it was
brokered by subduing the numens**: the mass binding of the world's living powers into
cores, trading caged divinity for **power, security, and comfort.** The uneasy peace IS the
cage-economy; civilization's lights are the terms of the armistice. Commonfolk don't even
call it a peace — war was the weather of generations, so a stretch without it is simply
**"the Calm"** *(Shane's coin)*. And now war is on the
horizon again. In this world, **numens are typed living power**: bound into salvaged
containment cores, they run what remains of civilization. The world is a fragile order
built on cages of captive power — **and the order and the cages are the same thing.**

## 2. The player — a mercenary (the spine)
You begin as **a nobody: a beginner mercenary scraping a living off the war's wreckage.**
Mercs are this world's adventurers-for-hire — they take contracts from anyone: loyal or
faithless, principled or self-serving, some good, some in it for money and power. You can
work your way into **any faction's good graces**, serve one, play several, or **walk your
own path.**

**Why the merc spine is the keystone (it's the connective tissue the design needed):**
- Justifies **faction alignment** — you don't "join" out of nowhere; you take contracts,
  build **reputation**, and a faction's path opens. That reputation *is* the cap-raising
  milestone spine (see WORLD_AND_CREATURES §10).
- Justifies **wandering/exploration** — mercs travel for work.
- Justifies the **ranch/haven** — the base is the home a survivor claws together in the
  hellscape (WORLD_AND_CREATURES §12). Earned, not given.
- Makes **morality the gameplay** — your choices in contracts define who you are. Grounded-
  dark tone as a mechanic, not a coat of paint.

## 3. The threat — two layers, one spine
Not two separate villains. A visible human antagonist with a cosmic horror behind it.

### 3a. The Obsidian Ledger (the visible antagonist — the player's dark mirror)
The oldest, largest mercenary syndicate: professionals who turned war into an **industry**,
playing factions against each other and profiting from perpetual conflict. Human, grounded,
and cold — debts and deaths written in something that doesn't wash out. Their leader is a
**legendary merc who chose the ledger over people** — literally what the player could
become. **The reveal, in layers:** the Ledger's leadership belongs to the Black Feast —
and beneath even that, its master is **the Founder himself, alive after centuries** (§3c);
they stoke war not just for profit but because *endless war serves the summoning.*

### 3b. The Black Feast (the cult) & the Maw Beneath (their god — the true, hidden threat)
- **The Maw Beneath:** a mad, chaotic, Cthulhu-grade god of **extinction-and-renewal** that
  none can contain and whose very presence **unmakes sanity.** It doesn't want to rule — it
  wants to **remake the world in a vision no sane mind can hold**: an ending that is also a
  beginning, death that is also new life. Its signature: it **feeds from shadow**, pulling
  things *in* from the dark beneath it (and so do its cultists' creatures). A hungry
  shadow-god, kept mostly a whisper — mystery over a named cartoon demon.
- **The Black Feast (cult)** *(renamed from "the Unmade" — Shane's coin, 2026-07-10; formal
  register "the Order of the Black Feast"):* they are **NOT deceived** — that's the horror. They *understand*
  the Maw and *want* it; they believe they'll be **transformed anew**, and offer their own
  creatures gladly to the same fate. Their belief is the dark inversion of the world's core
  lore: the entire order is an **obscenity — a planet of cages** (bound creatures), and the
  Maw will **open every cage at once.** War is the fuel: mass death + mass *unbinding* is
  the summoning. This ties the cosmic horror straight to the moral weight of catching
  creatures. And since the peace itself was bought with the mass binding, **opening every
  cage and reigniting the war are the SAME ACT** — the Feast's apocalypse is simply the
  world's true state resuming.

**The spine:** the Ledger is the Maw's instrument; the coming war is *designed.* The player
tangles with the Ledger for most of the game, then uncovers the cult behind it.

### 3c. The Founder — the war's buried origin (Shane, 2026-07-10)
The endless war was not fate. **Centuries ago — chronicles disagree; hundreds of years,
maybe a thousand — an old wardog of immense wealth, power, and intelligence grew BORED of
the peace of his age.** He craved a life of war, and he was capable enough to buy one. His
engineering of grievances, borders, and cages **triggered the war** whose wreckage is
Scoria. Commonfolk "no longer remember how it started" (§1) because the start was one
man's appetite, carefully hidden.

**LOCKED (Shane, 2026-07-10 — "the strong one, A"):**
- **He founded the Obsidian Ledger.** The black book began as HIS accounting of a purchased
  war; the syndicate is the oldest because it is his.
- **The Maw found him first.** Peace starves the Maw; one bored soldier's craving was the
  first door it ever whispered through. The Black Feast is his congregation — appetite
  organized into faith. The war's true name — **the Craving** — is HIS mood hidden in plain
  sight; commonfolk think "the Melancholy" describes how the era felt.
- **HE IS ALIVE.** The Maw's first bargain. The "legendary merc who chose the ledger over
  people" leading the Ledger TODAY is the Founder wearing centuries — the man who started
  the last war, personally starting the next, because he is bored again.
- Player-facing: a three-layer onion — the Ledger (visible) → the Black Feast (hidden) →
  the Founder (the buried truth) — delivered in whispers per §8 (mystery over
  hand-holding). Working epithet parked, NOT locked: "the Oldest Debt."

## 4. Campaign structure — ONE branching campaign, multiple endings
**Not** two separate campaigns (that's two games — the way to never ship). Instead: one
choice-driven campaign. The player's **alignment** (which factions, how ruthless) and a few
**key story beats** accumulate; the **back third forks**:
- **Stop the Black Feast** — rally what's left of the world against the summoning, or
- **Join the greater evil** — throw in with the cult and help open the cages.

Divergent climaxes and **multiple endings** (including the dark one). Replayability comes
from **choices & endings**, not duplicated content. **Every ending lands on the war
reigniting** (see §7). A full villain campaign, if ever, is far-future DLC — never a
release blocker.

## 5. Postgame (the collector-game endgame — keeps the genre intact)
- **The brutal arena** — wager money, fight escalating gauntlets for bigger purses; high
  risk/reward. Home for **opt-in flagged permadeath stakes**.
- **The gambling hall** — games of chance; win or lose your coin. On-theme war-town den.
- **Trainer/merc rematches** at cranked difficulty — the roster you built, tested at the top.
- **Hidden hunts** — secret areas; **mythic-rarity creatures and gear** as the ultimate
  chase; the ranch's rarest upgrades gated here.
- **Full free-roam exploration** once the story's done.

## 6. The factions (10) — personalities, creature theme, stance
Each faction has a **signature creature theme** (not exclusive). Exact element-mapping is a
roster-plan task (movement 3). Stance toward the coming war and the Maw noted.

**Shane's original five:**
1. **The Forge (Ember)** — iron pragmatists; military-industrial might; honor-in-strength;
   furnaces & war-frames. *Likely first mover in the war.* Leader: a hard, respected war-smith.
2. **The Arcane (Gale)** — proud aristocrat-scholars of storm-magic in old keeps; arrogant,
   secretive, hungry for forbidden knowledge → **the cult's easiest corruption target.**
   Leader: an aloof archmage-lord.
3. **The Bone (Cinder)** *(settled from "Blight/Bone" — Shane, 2026-07-10)* — grim, patient
   tenders of the dead; feared & reclusive — **but NOT the villains** (deliberate
   subversion: the creepy death-faction is innocent; the smiling cult is the rot). The
   **Barrow-King** haunts their edgelands. Leader: a somber warden.
4. **The Hive (Bloom)** — overgrown collectivist; fungal/insectoid; near-unknowable;
   expands by spreading; can't really be negotiated with. A wildcard nobody trusts. Leader:
   a hive-voice.
5. **The Drowned (Tide)** — mournful isolationists in flooded ruins; keepers of drowned
   history; insular, defensive, tradition-bound — **an ally you can earn if you court them.**
   Leader: a tidal matriarch.

**Claude's five:**
6. **The Choir (machines / robots / drones — the mech faction)** — where the Forge bolts
   salvage into brutal frames, the Choir builds **true machines**: sleek gundam-scale frames
   and **drone swarms** moving as one networked will. Cold, precise, almost serene; believe
   flesh is a flaw and the future is *built* — **technology as transcendence.** The clean
   mirror to the Forge's scrap-and-fury, and (quietly) the faction **most horrified by the
   Maw** (perfect order vs. a god of chaos). Leader: a half-machine architect-intelligence.
7. **The Ashborn (zealots / faith)** — survivors who found religion in the ashes; militant,
   ascetic, certain the war was judgment and they're the chosen remnant. **The cult's
   rivals, not its pawns** — a real human faith standing against the Maw. Righteous, but
   their certainty is frightening. Leader: a scarred prophet.
8. **The Wayfarers (nomads / caravans)** — no territory; roaming trade-convoys and outriders
   who cross everyone's land; neutral by necessity, information-rich. **A merc's natural
   early ally.** Leader: a caravan-master.
9. **The Concord (neutral hub / diplomats)** — the fragile institution that **brokered the
   peace**; neutral ground where the factions barely meet; idealistic, overstretched,
   quietly desperate. The **beating heart of "the peace is dying,"** and likely where the
   player first stumbles onto the Ledger's threads. When war comes, **the Concord's
   collapse is the point of no return.** Leader: a weary peace-broker.
10. **The Remnant (the Mutated)** — the living wreckage of forbidden war-science:
    amalgamations of human, creature, and worse, built as weapons and **discarded** when the
    war ended. Bound by shared monstrousness, carrying the grievances of the abandoned;
    **tragic and menacing.** The Maw's promise of remaking makes them its **most tempting
    recruits OR its fiercest enemies — and the player can tip which.** Their creatures are
    **chimeric / type-blending — hybridity is their signature** (the one faction that breaks
    the clean type mold). Leader: the first (or most coherent) amalgam.
    *(Names in reserve: The Sundered, The Discarded, The Castoffs.)*

**Rough web:** Forge & Hive are expansionist rivals; the Arcane plays aloof and gets
hollowed from within; the Bone stands alone and feared; the Drowned withdraws but can be
won; the Choir recoils from the Maw; the Ashborn crusade against it; the Wayfarers and
Concord try to hold the roads and the peace as it all frays. A tinderbox of tentative
alliances — exactly what the Black Feast needs to shatter into war.

## 7. The sequel seam (TABLED — not this game)
Shane's army/tactics vision — recruit/raise/gear/deploy troops (each a person + a creature,
XCOM/Fire-Emblem/Lost-Eidolon style), capture bases, reunlock the world by conquest or
alliance — is **a whole second game**, not a mode. **Every ending of this game lands on the
war reigniting**, which is the perfect sequel hook: Game 2 is *the war itself*, carrying
forward the player's save, creatures, and allegiance. **Tabled** until this game is done and
Shane has completed a playthrough. Recorded so it isn't lost.

## 8. Storytelling philosophy (canon — Shane)
**Mystery over hand-holding.** The game does NOT walk the player through every faction's
ideology or explain the ending. The player **infers** — from how a faction's creatures
behave, what they read and overhear, the contracts they take, who they fight and why. Not
*incomprehensible* — but never a walkthrough. **Trust the player to be smart.** This shapes
every quest, line, and reveal.

## 9. World map & starting point (movement 2 — LOCKED)
**Shape:** a **neutral heart (the Concord)** at center; the territories radiate around it;
the **Wayfarers' roads thread between** everyone (they hold no land — they ARE the roads);
and the **Remnant** are scattered in the war's scarred margins (dead zones, forbidden
test-sites). **Key adjacencies / flashpoints:** the **Forge↔Choir** border (two visions of
technology glaring across a line) and the **Forge↔Bone frontier.** (Visual: `world_map.html`.)

**Starting point — the tavern (Shane):** the adventure begins in a **tavern in Emberfall**,
a hardscrabble frontier town on the Forge/Bone border — the merc's natural origin
(contract board, rumor mill, first crew). The player starts a nobody, takes a first
contract, gets their starter, and heads out the **Fell-Grass Route** toward **The Hollow**
(Bone edgelands; the Barrow-King). This IS the demo already built — now with a place in
the world and a reason to walk out the door.

**Terrain & landmarks (the wild between the strongholds — Shane + Claude):** the factions
are **strongholds scattered across a wilderness**; the space between is where a merc lives,
and where exploration/wild creatures/secrets happen. The *vocabulary* is locked; the
*specific* geography of each region is drawn **per-region as we build it** (Pallet Town, not
all of Kanto). Biomes are the **first input to the roster** (creatures come from places).
- **Biomes:** ashen wastes · deep forest · fungal jungle · high mountains · marsh & wetland
  · flooded coast & open water · storm-scoured highlands · **dead zones** (cursed war-scars,
  Remnant country).
- **Landmarks:** war-relics & rusted battlefields · pre-war ruins · **hidden labs** (where the
  Remnant were made; rare creatures & secrets) · **independent enclaves** (isolated survivor-
  pockets, each warped by its own strange tech/magic/curse — the *feel* of Fallout vaults
  minus the cruelty; a merc's waystations & mystery-boxes) · **enemy holdfasts** (Ledger
  safehouses, cult shrines).
- Visual: `world_map_v2.html` (strongholds spaced out, biomes filling the between).
