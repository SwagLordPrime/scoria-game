## 1. World identity (Shane's vision, this session)

**The pitch:** a grounded, quietly dark world of **techno-organic decay laced with
fantasy and old magic.** Not a bright pastoral meadow — a post-war landscape where dead
machines, bone, nature, and arcane forces have all rotted and grown together. Rusted
war-husks half-buried in tall grass. Bone in the underbrush. Medieval castles beside
infested hives beside dead technology. Reference feel: the melancholy techno-decay of
*NieR* / *Horizon* and the restrained dread of *Shin Megami Tensei* — muted, overgrown,
a little wrong. Grounded, NOT edgelord (rust and rain and silence, not chrome and gore).

**Magical / fantasy elements are explicitly wanted** alongside the tech and decay —
castles, old magic, arcane and eldritch aspects all belong.

## 2. The premise & structure (Shane's idea — and it's the game's backbone)

**Premise:** *post-war, an uneasy attempt at peace.* Different **factions** each hold
their own territory. The war left the world littered with machines and bone; now
everyone warily rebuilds. (The vagueness is a feature — it explains the decay, bends any
direction, and gives us cool lore for free.)

**Structure (the Pokémon-town/gym analogue, but better):**
- Each **region = a faction's territory**, with its own themed creatures, look, and vibe.
- Each region's **faction leader = the "gym leader"** — a territory's ruler, not a
  sports-badge boss. Confronting them is the regional milestone.
- This solves world coherence (every place has a *reason* to exist) and scales cleanly
  (a new region = a new faction, fully themed).

**Faction ↔ type map (north-star framework — each of the 5 types gets a home):**
| Faction (working) | Flavor | Type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Forge** | War-machines, furnaces, salvaged tech | Ember |
| **The Arcane / Castle** | Medieval castles, old storm-magic | Gale |
| **The Bone** | Decay, drain, the reassembling dead | Cinder |
| **The Hive** | Bug, infested, overgrown, fungal | Bloom |
| **The Drowned** | Flooded ruins, bells, still water | Tide |
| *(beyond the edges)* | Genuinely eldritch / unknown — *answered: Umbra & Aether* | ✓ |

Names were placeholders when written — **now LOCKED; the sheet of record is `NAMES.md`**
(ten factions, all places, the war/peace/cult, the lot). Wording of "faction leader" still TBD.

**Scope note:** factions, castles, leaders — all **north-star, post-V1.** V1 is one town
+ a couple routes + one mini-boss. This map is just where we're headed so every small
piece points somewhere.

## 5. Mechanics locked earlier this session (cross-ref DESIGN_OVERVIEW)

- **Movement:** grid-locked, tile-by-tile.
- **Party:** 6 carried + a storage box (box can be minimal in V1).
- **Swap in battle:** costs your turn (the core tactical tension).
- **Working V1 assumptions (confirm as we build):** single type per creature; fixed
  movesets (no move-learning in V1).
- **Progression — Path 3 (Shane's call):** team-building **now** — the player gets stronger
  by catching a diverse, well-typed roster and mastering the wheel, NOT by grinding
  levels. Level is cosmetic in V1. A real **leveling/XP system** (stat growth on level-up,
  XP from battles, possibly move-learning) is canon but its **own dedicated later phase** —
  not bolted on. This is *why* the Phase-4 gate boss is a type puzzle, not a stat wall.

## 6. The containment core — living power (Shane's canon, this session)

**What you catch creatures with, and what it MEANS.** No "Poké Balls" — that would
shatter the grounded tone. Instead: a **salvaged containment core** — a rune-etched
canister of war-scrap repurposed with old magic. You weaken a wild creature, then bind
it into the core (tech + arcane in one object, tying the machines and the magic together).

**The idea that reframes the whole premise (Shane):** the cores are *how the world is
(partially) powered.* A creature bound in a core is a **living power source**, and what
it yields **depends on its type** — an Ember creature runs a furnace/forge hot, a Gale
creature drives things that move or spark, Tide/Cinder each give different power. The
creatures aren't just collectibles — they're the batteries civilization runs on.

**Why this is huge:**
- It deepens the **factions**: the Forge hoards Ember-cores, the Arcane bind storm-things
  — the post-war scramble is partly a fight over *who holds the living batteries that keep
  the lights on.*
- It adds quiet **moral weight**: catching = binding a living thing into a cell to power
  your journey. Grounded, a little dark, on-tone.
- Catching is **unlimited** in V1 (tension comes from weaken-vs-faint risk + a failed
  attempt costing your turn, not item hoarding). Core scarcity/economy = later layer.

→ **Locked as core canon.** Base catch device = salvaged containment core; "creatures as
typed living power" is now part of the world's premise.

## 7. Player as combatant — RPG gear (canon; mechanic post-V1)

Shane wants the **player to be an active combatant**, not an invisible hand — an
RPG-style character who **equips gear** and fights alongside their creatures (SMT/Persona
flavor, fits Shane's taste). Canon for the world; the **mechanic is post-V1** (real
scope). Consequence for design: the "trainer" is a *character with a kit*, and eventual
enemy "faction leaders" are too.

## 8. Stakes & mortality (canon; post-V1)

Shane wants **real stakes — a creature's life can be permanently lost — but ONLY at
climactic/intense moments** (e.g. immediately before a major boss), and **always with
advance warning to the player.** NOT global permadeath in routine fights.

- This deliberately sidesteps the savescum / bad-luck-wipe problem that global permadeath
  would cause in a persistent-save open world.
- Design shape: a **flagged high-stakes encounter** (telegraphed) where death is real;
  everywhere else stays faint → recover. Possibly a wound/near-death state before true
  death so it's tense but fair.
- **Post-V1.** V1 keeps faint → black out → wake at safe point (§5 of DESIGN_OVERVIEW).

## 9. Bosses

### The Barrow-King — first named boss (Phase 4; gate of The Hollow)

The gate guardian: a towering, reassembled bone-titan — the wild apex of the same dead
that Marrowling crawls from. Ancient, buried, dreadful (grounded folklore dread, NOT gore).
**Cinder / Bruiser, boss-tier.**

- **Role in the game:** the gate that *proves team-building* (Path 3). Cinder beats Ember,
  so leading with the player's Slagling starter gets it mauled; winning requires **Bloom
  (Mireling)** or **Gale (Fenmoth)**. A mono-starter player hits a wall; a collector who
  built a diverse team walks through. This one fight teaches the whole game.
- **Signature — Lifedrain (escalates by its OWN HP, not player deaths):** heals 33% of
  damage dealt while above 50% HP, **43% at/below 50%** ("feeding frenzy"). Escalation is a
  climactic **damage-check** on a winning player, deliberately NOT a death-spiral off an
  unlucky faint (the per-kill version was considered and rejected for exactly that reason).
  Self-contained boss rule behind a flag — not a general ability system.
- **Defeat-only** — cannot be caught ("no core can bind the Barrow-King").
- **Stats (starting, tunable):** HP 75, Atk 13, Def 9, Spd 4; moves *Marrow Drain*
  (Cinder, 7) and *Grave Sunder* (Neutral, 6), both trigger drain. Full build spec:
  `PHASE4_BUILD_PLAN.md`.
- **Naming:** named by Claude at Shane's request. *'Fleshrender'* was rejected — flesh
  imagery misfires on a bone creature and tips into the edgelord register we ruled out.
  Reserve alts: *Gravemarrow*, *Hollowmarch*.

## 10. Progression, XP & faction alignment (canon)

Path 3's leveling system is the **next build phase**. Shane's calls:

- **Shared XP.** The whole party levels together (no babysitting a single creature) —
  keeps a diverse bench viable, on-theme for a collector. *Working default (confirm in
  build):* every non-fainted party member gets full shared XP; fainted get none.
- **Per-stat growth curve (Pokémon-style), kept simple to start.** Each stat grows with
  level per creature; a fancier curve can come later.
- **Move-learning at set levels: YES, but a LATER phase.** One system at a time — this
  phase is levels + XP + stat growth only; movesets stay fixed (2 moves) for now.

### Level cap gated by milestones — the "badge" equivalent (Shane's key idea)
Creatures level only up to a **cap**, and the cap is raised by clearing progression gates
— this game's version of a gym badge. **In the full game, that gate is defeating a
faction's LEADER — OR, if the player chooses to ALIGN with that faction, defeating the
leader's RIVAL on their behalf instead.**

This introduces a **branching faction-allegiance system**: the player can pick a side in
the post-war cold war. Independent → fight the leader; aligned → fight their rival and
serve the faction. Who you serve changes who you fight (and, later, presumably what you
gain). This makes the "uneasy peace between factions" premise *participatory*. **Canon,
post-V1.**

- **V1 mapping:** the only V1 gate is the **Barrow-King**, so it stands in as V1's
  cap-raising milestone. Build the cap as **milestone-driven** (a cap value raised by a
  cleared-gate flag) so faction leaders/rivals slot into that same mechanism later.
- **Numbers (Shane):** V1 level **cap 15**; the Barrow-King sits at **~level 18** — it
  out-levels the player's cap on purpose, reinforcing that **type beats levels.**
- **⚠ Consequence:** adding leveling changes ALL combat math, so the **Barrow-King must be
  re-tuned / re-simmed** under leveling to preserve its verified intent (Ember-only loses,
  Bloom/Gale wins, tense). Its current flat-stat tuning (Atk 8 / HP 75) will change.

2. How is alignment chosen, and what are its consequences/rewards?
3. Shared-XP details (benched share confirmed; fainted share? split vs full?).
4. Exact XP curve, per-stat growth rates, and how the cap steps up per milestone.

## 11. Evolution, Temper & Move-learning (full design)

The official progression design. **Built in layers** (see 11.7) — the demo ships the
level-based slice; the deep layers follow once their dependencies exist.

### 11.1 The Temper meters — Bond & Wrath (signature system)
Two universal meters per creature, each **0–100**, tracked **independently** ("Temper" =
the screen/system name, provisional):
- **Bond** — warmth, loyalty, trust.
- **Wrath** — rancor, barely-held fury.
Independent, so a creature can be high **both** (a loyal berserker).
- **Bond ↑:** keeping it in your party, healing it, ranch time (passive over time), a light
  **treat/food** system (active), winning without it fainting.
- **Wrath ↑:** frequent fainting, relentless grinding, neglect (no treats / no ranch time).
- **Wrath's teeth:** past a Wrath threshold, the creature deals a **scaling % of chip damage
  to the HERO** on its own hits (scales with Wrath 0–100). The hero need NOT be on the
  field — the hero sits in the lineup with an **HP pool**, and a wrathful active creature
  erodes it. (Needs only Bond/Wrath tracking + a hero HP pool — decoupled from the full
  gear/hero-combatant pillar.) The savage path is stronger but bites the hand that holds it.

### 11.2 Branching evolution — the first-evo race (Shane's model)
At the **first evolution**, the branch is decided by **which condition crosses its
threshold FIRST** as the creature reaches evolution level:
- **Bond** first → **Bond branch** (serene, protective form)
- **Wrath** first → **Wrath branch** (feral, savage form)
- **Neither** crossed by evo level (plain leveling) → **Neutral branch** (default line)
- **Both high / a creature-unique condition** → **specialty / hidden branch** (rare only)

→ 3 core paths + hidden = "2–4 lines from how you raised it." Each branch continues to its
**own final form** (base → chosen 1st-evo → that branch's final) so lines don't
combinatorially explode. **Most common creatures:** simple single line or none. **Starters
+ a rare few:** the full depth.

### 11.3 Creature-unique third meters (starters & rares only — Claude's to design)
On top of Bond/Wrath, starters and a rare few get ONE thematic third gauge that unlocks a
hidden branch or secret final form. Seeds: mech's furnace **Overload**, wolf's parasitic
**Signal**, skeleton's bone **Accretion**. Everything else has none.

### 11.4 Move-learning — two lanes
- **Innate:** learned automatically at **set levels** (Pokémon-style; the creature's nature
  emerging as it grows).
- **Forged:** **crafted** into a creature at the ranch **forge** using materials ranging
  **common → mythic**, scaled to the move's power/rarity (Shane). Deeper than a TM; an
  economy sink; on-theme (you *forge* a move in, you don't teach it). Full forge mechanic
  TBD when the ranch/economy exist.
- **Slot caps (LOCKED):** 3-stage line **2 → 3 → 4**; single-evolution (2-stage) **3 → 4**;
  non-evolving **4**; ultra-rare **5**. Learning into a full set → replace-prompt.

### 11.5 On evolving
Stat **jump** (each stage has its own higher base stats; level-scaling applies off them),
**keeps its moves**, option to learn a **signature move**, **new look**, **new passive**.

### 11.6 Passives
- Stat passives (+Atk; or trade-offs like ++Atk/–Def), **status-on-hit** (bleed / poison /
  burn / rot / others), and **positive buffs** (per-turn regen, +max-HP at battle start, a
  shield). *(Status-on-hit needs a status-effect framework the demo lacks — designed now,
  built later.)*
- **Starters get a passive LINE** that evolves with them. Only creatures where it fits get
  passives.
- **The exception faction (Shane):** ONE type/faction has **no passives**, compensating via
  raw stats / typing / movepool — a deliberate identity. Should be a **non-starter type**
  (Tide or Bloom) so starters keep their passive lines; exact faction chosen in the world pass.

### 11.7 Dependency & build order
Deep layers depend on systems not yet built (Bond/Wrath tracking, the ranch + treats, the
forge + economy, a status framework, a hero HP pool). So:
- **Demo (next build — Phase 6):** **level-based evolution + innate level move-learning +
  slot caps + stat jumps**, on the reachable lines (**Slagling**, **Marrowling**). This IS
  the Neutral branch by definition (no meters yet).
- **Later, layered, in order:** ranch → Bond/Wrath + treats → branching (Bond/Wrath/specialty
  variant forms) → forge / crafted moves → statuses → passives → hero HP + Wrath-damage →
  third meters. Content (variant forms, passive lines) is fleshed out against the world pass.

## 12. The Ranch — the player's haven (north-star pillar)

**Shane's vision (a capstone pillar):** the player earns and builds their own base — a
**creature ranch** — that serves as their **"haven in the hellscape."** The emotional
anchor of the game: a warm, safe, cared-for home that contrasts the grounded, war-scarred
world outside. The hellscape hits harder *because* you have somewhere soft to protect and
return to.

**How it's built — EARNED, not freely customized.** Rather than open customization, ranch
upgrades are **earned** through play: achievements, quest rewards, enemy drops, wild
finds, chests, and store purchases. Each upgrade is unique; there are likely themed
**sets** that synergize, but the player can **mix and match** freely.

**Activities — raise / breed / release / harvest.** A gentle creature ranch:
- **Raise & breed** your bound creatures.
- **Release** creatures (purpose TBD — compendium credit? frees box space? a materials or
  goodwill payoff?).
- **Harvest** materials for **crafting** — **STRICTLY NON-CRUEL.** Hard rule (Shane): think
  shearing wool from a sheep — net-neutral or *beneficial* to the creature, never harmful.
  **Nothing cruel ever happens at the player's ranch.** It is a sanctuary. This is a
  tone/ethics guardrail, not a suggestion.

**Why it's a LATE pillar (scope reality):** the ranch is a **capstone that sits on top of
several systems we don't have yet** — items/inventory, crafting, an economy (drops, chests,
shops), breeding, and an achievement framework. It's where those foundations converge.
So it's genuinely post-V1 and comes *after* those substrates exist — but it's a superb
north-star that gives all that groundwork a destination.
